 The Idea 

One Form. A Whole Backend.

Most creators think a lead magnet needs a dozen moving parts. It doesn't. It needs one entry point — and a quiet system behind it that sorts, stores, and follows up on its own.

A funnel doesn't have to feel like a machine with a hundred gears. The simplest version of a backend is really just one question people answer, and a handful of tools that already know what to do with the answer.

That's the whole idea behind the MiniApp OS Funnel Map. A single form becomes the front door. Everything that happens after — the routing, the storage, the follow-up email — runs quietly in the background, without you touching it after the first setup.

The value isn't in any one tool. It's in the hand-off between them. Each piece does one job, passes the result to the next piece, and gets out of the way. That's what makes it feel effortless once it's live.

 The Build 

Five Pieces, One System

1
Tally Form — The Front Door
One short form is where every relationship starts. A few well-chosen questions do double duty: they capture an email and tell you exactly who's on the other end — their pain point, their interest, their next step.
2
n8n Router — The Traffic Controller
The moment a form is submitted, n8n picks it up and decides where it goes. No manual sorting, no forgetting to follow up — the routing logic runs the same way every single time.
3
Notion Vault — The Memory
Every lead, answer, and idea gets a permanent home. Instead of insights disappearing into a form's dashboard, they sit in a living database you can search, sort, and mine for your next product.
4
Beehiiv / MailerLite — The Relationship
A new subscriber is added and dropped into a welcome sequence automatically. This is where a one-time visitor turns into someone who opens your emails and eventually buys.
5
Future Product — The Payoff
Every answer stored in the vault is market research you didn't have to ask for twice. Patterns in what people say become the next template, guide, or tool you build — already validated before you make it.
 Getting Started 

Build Your Own Version

You don't need all five pieces on day one. Start with the form, and add the rest as you need it.

1
Build one form
Keep it to three to five questions. Every question should tell you something you can act on later.
2
Decide where each answer should live
Map every field to a destination before you automate anything — a database row, a tag, a list.
3
Automate the hand-off
Connect the form to your router so new submissions move on their own, with no manual step in between.
4
Write one welcome email
A single, honest welcome message is enough to start turning subscribers into a warm audience.
5
Let it run — then read the vault
Check back in a few weeks. The patterns in what people ask for are your next product roadmap.
